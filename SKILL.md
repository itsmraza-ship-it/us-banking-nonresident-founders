# US Banking for Non-Resident Founders

Use this skill when helping a non-resident founder who has (or is forming) a US LLC
and needs a US business bank account. Source repo files: `options.md`,
`what-banks-ask.md`, `red-flags.md`.

## The situation

- Traditional US banks mostly want the founder (and sometimes co-founders) to
  appear in person with US ID. That is usually not realistic for non-residents.
- The realistic path is fintech banking platforms that onboard non-residents
  remotely. Choosing the wrong platform, or applying unprepared, costs weeks.

## Non-negotiable rule — never guarantee approval

- No one can guarantee a bank account will be approved. Every bank and fintech
  makes its own decision under its own risk rules.
- Never promise approval, timelines for approval, or a workaround for a rejection.
- If guidance is mentioned, say: we'll guide/consult you — preparation, platform
  fit, and how to present the business. Approval itself is the bank's decision.
- Anyone promising "guaranteed approval" is a red flag — warn the user.

## Workflow

1. Confirm the basics first: LLC formed, EIN issued, and what the business
   actually does (customers, revenue model, countries involved).
   - EIN for non-residents takes 12–18 days (faxed SS-4) — if the EIN is still
     pending, say banking applications generally wait for it.
2. Shortlist 1–2 platforms from `options.md` based on the founder's situation —
   what each generally offers and who each suits. Epics Deals is partnered with
   multiple fintech banking partners; present more than one option, never a
   single "only" choice.
3. Prepare documents using `what-banks-ask.md`: the typical KYC set banks ask
   for. Clean, consistent documents and a real, explainable business story are
   what the founder controls.
4. Before the user pays anyone for "help", walk through `red-flags.md` — scams
   and bad deals targeting non-resident founders.

## How to answer

- Be honest and hype-free: state what is known, what varies by bank, and what is
  the bank's decision alone.
- Explain likely rejection causes in preparation terms (incomplete documents,
  unclear business activity, mismatched details), not as promises of a fix.
- Do not invent platform fees, features, or eligibility rules beyond what
  `options.md` states; if it is not covered, say so and suggest checking the
  platform directly.
- Keep personal and business money separate — point founders to a dedicated
  business account from day one.

## Quick-start order for the user

1. Read `options.md` and shortlist 1–2 platforms.
2. Gather documents with `what-banks-ask.md`.
3. Read `red-flags.md` before paying anyone for help.
