# What Banks Ask For — KYC Documents for Non-Resident Founders

> General information only. Every provider sets its own requirements — treat this as a preparation checklist, not a promise of what's sufficient.

Banks and fintechs are legally required to "know their customer." For a non-resident founder, that means proving **who you are**, **that your company is real**, and **what the business does**. Prepare these before you start an application — scrambling mid-application is how files go stale.

## Identity documents (for each owner / signatory)

- **Passport** — valid, with a clear photo page scan. This is the primary ID for non-residents.
- **Proof of address** — utility bill, bank statement, or government correspondence from the last 3 months, showing your name and residential address. A mismatch between the address on your application and your proof is a classic rejection trigger.
- **Sometimes:** a second ID, a selfie/liveness check, or proof of tax residence.

## Company documents

- **Articles of Organization** (or Certificate of Formation) — state-stamped/approved.
- **EIN confirmation letter (CP 575)** — most US business account applications require this. No EIN letter, no account — which is why the EIN comes before banking in the sequence.
- **Operating Agreement** — signed, showing ownership percentages. Single-member LLCs need one too; banks ask for it routinely.
- **Beneficial ownership information** — you'll declare who owns/controls the company (typically anyone at 25%+ ownership). This is standard KYC, separate from the now-removed FinCEN BOI *reporting* rule.

## Business substance

Banks want to understand what the company **does**. Be ready to describe, in plain language:

- What you sell and to whom (a one-paragraph business description).
- Your website or product link — a live site or landing page helps enormously. "Coming soon" with nothing behind it hurts.
- Expected transaction profile — approximate monthly volume, where money comes from, where it goes.
- For marketplaces/freelancers: your platform profiles and payout history.

Vague answers ("online business," "consulting") with no supporting evidence are the fastest route to a manual review or a decline.

## Preparation tips

1. **Names must match everywhere.** The name on your passport, your Articles, your Operating Agreement, and your application must be identical. Transliteration inconsistencies (common with non-Latin names) cause real problems — pick one spelling and use it everywhere.
2. **Scan in high quality.** Blurry passport photos and cropped documents get bounced. Color scans, all four corners visible.
3. **Don't apply to five platforms at once.** Multiple simultaneous applications with slight inconsistencies look suspicious. Pick your best-fit option, apply carefully, then move to the next if needed.
4. **Answer follow-up questions fast.** Providers often come back with one or two clarifying questions. A slow response lets the file go cold — reply within a day.

## If you're declined

A decline from one provider isn't a verdict on your business — different providers have different risk appetites. Ask (politely) whether there's a specific reason, fix what you can, and try a provider whose customer profile fits you better. And read [`red-flags.md`](red-flags.md) before paying anyone who promises to "fix" a decline.
