# Fintech Banking Options for Non-Resident Founders

> General information only, not financial advice. Features, fees, and eligibility change — verify on each provider's site before applying. **Account approval is always subject to the provider's own decision.**

## Why fintech instead of a traditional bank

Most traditional US banks require an in-person visit and, in practice, a US-resident signatory. A handful work with non-residents remotely, but it's the exception. Fintech platforms built for startups and global businesses are the realistic remote path: online onboarding, USD accounts with routing + account numbers, debit cards, and integrations with Stripe, PayPal, and accounting tools.

None of the summaries below are endorsements or guarantees — they're starting points for your own comparison.

---

## The options, by category

### Startup-focused business banking

**Mercury**
- Built specifically for startups, including non-US founders.
- USD checking and savings, virtual + physical debit cards, free domestic wires on many plans.
- Known for a smooth remote onboarding flow and startup-friendly integrations (accounting, cap-table tools).
- Generally requires your EIN and formation documents; approval is at Mercury's discretion.

**Relay**
- Business banking aimed at small businesses, with strong multi-account budgeting features.
- USD accounts, debit cards, integrations with accounting software.
- Worth comparing on fees and limits for your transaction profile.

### Global / multi-currency platforms

**Airwallex**
- Built for cross-border businesses: multi-currency accounts, international transfers, and spend management.
- Strong if you invoice or pay across several currencies, not just USD.
- Business verification requirements apply; approval at their discretion.

**Payoneer**
- Long-established cross-border payments network, popular with freelancers and e-commerce sellers.
- Local-currency receiving accounts in multiple countries plus USD capabilities.
- Good fit if your money flows are marketplace/freelance-heavy rather than classic startup banking.

**Wise (Wise Business)**
- Multi-currency account with transparent mid-market exchange rates.
- Useful as a complement to a primary USD account — especially for converting and moving money internationally at fair rates.
- Not a full bank (it's an e-money institution in most regions) — understand what that means for your needs.

## How to choose

| If you… | Look hardest at |
|---|---|
| Run a funded/startup-style company, need clean books + integrations | Startup-focused banking (Mercury, Relay) |
| Operate across many currencies | Multi-currency platforms (Airwallex, Wise) |
| Get paid via marketplaces / freelance platforms | Cross-border networks (Payoneer) |
| Want the lowest FX cost on transfers | Compare mid-market-rate providers (Wise) side by side |

Most founders end up with **two**: a primary USD business account and a multi-currency tool for FX. That's normal.
